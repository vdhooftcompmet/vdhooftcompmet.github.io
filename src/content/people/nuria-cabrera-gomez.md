Nuria obtained her BSc in Biotechnology from the University of Cádiz (Spain) in 2021, followed by an MSc in Genetics and Evolution from the University of Granada in 2022. In 2023, she started her PhD as a predoctoral researcher in the Department of Organic Chemistry at the University of Cádiz, where she joined the "Diseño Biosintético de Fungicidas" group at the Institute of Biomolecules (INBIO), under the supervision of Prof. Josefina Aleu Casatejada and Prof. Isidro González Collado.

Her PhD research focuses on fungal secondary metabolism in the necrotrophic plant pathogen Botrytis cinerea and its contribution to pathogenicity. Her work combines chemical, biological, and genetic approaches to investigate fungal metabolite production and biosynthesis. She applies One Strain–Many Compounds (OSMAC) strategies through controlled fermentations and extraction procedures, characterises fungal metabolites using NMR spectroscopy and high-resolution mass spectrometry, and investigates the genetic basis of eremophilane-type sesquiterpene biosynthesis through targeted gene knockout approaches, with a particular focus on the stc7 gene cluster.

During her predoctoral research stay at Wageningen University & Research, Nuria joined the Computational Metabolomics group in the Department of Plant Sciences, under the supervision of Dr. Justin van der Hooft and postdoctoral researcher Dr. Larissa Della Vedova. Her project, “Molecular networking in Botrytis cinerea: linking metabolism to phenotype and genetic variation,” focuses on the application of Feature-Based Molecular Networking (FBMN) through GNPS2, together with computational metabolomics tools for metabolite annotation and prioritisation, including SpecReBoot, Retromol and FERMO.

Through this work, she aims to explore condition-dependent metabolic plasticity, compare the metabolomic profiles of phenotypically distinct B. cinerea strains and investigate the functional roles of candidate genes involved in eremophilene biosynthesis.

Her main research interests include computational metabolomics, fungal secondary metabolism, feature-based molecular networking and plant–pathogen interactions.
